# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

League of Legends players on the LAS server (Argentina first), mostly young, competitive, playing ranked at night. They arrive from an Instagram story ad or a link shared in a Discord, on their phone, between games. The job: decide in a few seconds whether this week's cup is for them, then get ready to enter with their team. A secondary audience is the Riot developer-relations reviewer who opens the public URL to check that the product is real, legal and clearly not an official Riot property.

## Product Purpose

Gorosei hosts recurring LAS tournaments, at least one every week, with Riot-verified identities, rank-based eligibility, live brackets and streamed matches. It exists because Battlefy is effectively dead for LAS and there is no Spanish-language, LAS-first place where a tournament is always open. Success is a visitor who understands who can play, what it is, when, where and what the prize is within a few seconds, and who creates an account and links Riot so they can register the day it opens.

## Positioning

There is always a cup open for LAS, and it feels like the broadcast, not a form.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: get ready for the cup. Signed out: create an account; signed in: link the Riot account; linked: a confirmation that they are ready.
- Secondary: the player's own public profile once linked.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: Copa Semanal, this Saturday, and the prize amount.
- Belief ladder: this is a real tournament for my server → I can play at my rank → the date and prize are concrete → the prize is the real pool of entry fees, no surprises → it takes a minute to be ready.
- Proof on hand: none yet; the first edition is the proof. No testimonials or logos to show.

## Brand Personality

Direct, competitive, warm. It speaks rioplatense Spanish with "vos", like a teammate calling the play, never like a bank. The emotional goal is the competitive rush of the moment a broadcast goes live: the pull of the stage, the trophy and the prize, before the visitor has tapped anything.

## Anti-references

- The generic AI landing page: card grids, purple gradients, gradient text, glassmorphism, a tiny uppercase eyebrow above every section.
- Anything that looks official: Riot, Worlds or LoL Esports logos, Summoner's Cup imagery, champion splash art, Riot marks.
- Battlefy's interchangeable listing pages: a tournament as a row in a table.
- A page that is exciting but unclear: hype without the date, the rank cap or the fee.

## Design Principles

1. Five Ws in five seconds: who, what, when, where and the prize are readable on a phone without scrolling far.
2. Broadcast, not brochure: the page borrows the language of a live esports broadcast (stage light, lower-third caption bar, countdown) and makes it Gorosei's own.
3. No surprises about money: the fee and the "pool is the prize" line always sit together.
4. Red means live: signal red is spent only on the live/status tag and the one call to action.
5. Content first, motion second: everything is readable server-rendered with no JavaScript; motion only enhances.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA: body text at least 4.5:1, large text at least 3:1, visible focus on every interactive element, every animation has a prefers-reduced-motion alternative, and no content is ever gated on an animation or on JavaScript. Mobile over Argentine networks is the baseline: no hero media, self-hosted subset fonts.
